The **Network Addon Mod** (**NAM**) is a mod improving and expanding upon the functionality of the [SimCity 4](/simcity-4) [transit networks](/transit-network), produced by the [NAM Team](/nam/members). The most recent stable version, Version 50, was released on August 18, [2026](/2026).

For information on the current release of the NAM and where to download go to [NAM Plugin](/plugin/nam). For NAM User Guide pages which are up to date with the latest NAM release go to [the online NAM Documentation](https://sc4devotion.com/namdoc).

## Development History

The NAM project was founded by [redlotus](/people/redlotus), [the7trumpets](/people/the7trumpets) and [Tropod](/people/tropod), part of [Modd Squad Transport](/modd-squad) (now the [NAM Team](/nam-team)) in [2004](/2004), with Version 1 being released on April 24, 2004. Tropod headed up most of the early development on the project, earning him the moniker "Mr. NAM", up until the Version 19 release in October [2005](/2005). In its initial form, it largely consisted of a series of bugfixes and a variety of [Traffic Simulator](/traffic-simulator) plugin options to improve pathfinding, as well as a few new transit items. As the mod progressed, the focus shifted toward the creation of new transit items, beginning with the viaduct overpass puzzle pieces and new interchanges for [Maxis Highways](/maxis-highways). Going into 2005, the [Ground Light Rail](/ground-light-rail) (GLR) system was added, as well as several new draggable items, including Diagonal Streets, the Road, One-Way Road and Street Roundabout Plugins, and the Road and Avenue Turning Lane Plugins, as well as the Elevated Rail-above-Road dual-network puzzle pieces.

Between October 2005 and the end of 2006, the project went into a hiatus from public activity, but was revived by several [SFBT](/sfbt) members, including [memo](/people/memo), [Andreas](/people/andreas), [GoaSkin](/people/goaskin), and [ArkenbergeJoe](/people/arkenbergejoe), along with a few other prior team members, resulting in the release of Version 20 in December [2006](/2006), which introduced the Diagonal Street Helper Pieces (the first instance of a [WAVERide](/waveride) setup), and the [URail](/urail) system.

With Version 21, the June [2007](/2007) release, the NAM was "modularized", separating the main [RUL Files](/rul-file) that made the mod function out into a separate NAM Controller file. This was done in order to prevent various conflicts that had occurred, such as the NAM Version 20/[RHW](/rhw) Version 1.2 conflict in early 2007, and to allow for easier updating of [NAM Plugins](/nam-plugins). This also marked the start of the NAM Optional Plugins program, beginning with Version 1.3b of the then-[Rural Highway Mod](/rural-highway-mod) being released concurrently with the June 2007 NAM, and the first release of the [Street Addon Mod](/street-addon-mod) in September 2007. Version 21 also marked the introduction of Draggable GLR, the first [Override Network](/override-network), as well as the Avenue Roundabouts, Tram-in-Avenue, and Road Wide-Radius Curve puzzle pieces.

After Version 21, the team's personnel ranks expanded considerably. The next release, Version 22, released January 1, 2008, was not released as a full NAM version, but rather, used an "update" package, through which the user could simply update their Version 21 files. The subsequent Version 23 release returned to the full package paradigm, and brought several new traffic simulator options developed by [jplumbley](/people/jplumbley) and [mott](/people/mott) to the NAM, reflecting the latest research.

Beginning with Version 24 in January 2009, much of the new content being added to the NAM itself was by the very productive SFBT unit led by [Chrisim](/people/chrisim), producing the [Tram-Road Addon Mod](/tram-road-addon-mod), [Flexible Underpasses](/flexible-underpasses), and [URail](/urail) content. [Fractional Angle](/fractional-angle) Roads and Railroads, a project of [dedgren](/people/dedgren) and [Tarkus](/people/tarkus) also debuted here. 2009 would see 3 more NAM releases, in March (Version 25, concurrent with the initial [Rail Addon Mod](/rail-addon-mod) (RAM) release), June (Version 26, the first release of the [Canal Addon Mod](/canal-addon-mod)), and a small update in July (Version 27). 2009 also saw the inclusion of the first new Maxis Highway interchanges since 2005, courtesy of [andreharv](/people/andreharv).

There were two releases in 2010, including Version 28 in May, which marked the introduction of the [NAM Unified Traffic Simulator](/nam-unified-traffic-simulator) and related [Traffic Simulator Configuration Tool](/traffic-simulator-configuration-tool) (TSCT), the [Turn Lane Extension Pieces](/turn-lane-extension-pieces) (TuLEPS) and the initial offering of the long-awaited [Network Widening Mod](/network-widening-mod) (NWM), followed by Version 29 in August.

In September 2011, Version 30 was released, concurrent with RealHighway Version 5.0 and Network Widening Mod Version 2.0.

The NAM Team decided earlier in 2011 to move away from the existing "Modular NAM" concept and toward adopting a "Monolithic NAM" paradigm for NAM Version 31. After the "Monolithic NAM" was implemented, all previous major "external" NAM plugins, such as the [RealHighway](/realhighway), [Network Widening Mod](/network-widening-mod), [Rail Addon Mod](/rail-addon-mod), [High Speed Rail Project](/high-speed-rail-project), [Street Addon Mod](/street-addon-mod) and [Rural Roads Plugin](/rural-roads-plugin), were merged into the NAM Core itself, simplifying the package considerably.

For the first and only time, the NAM Team announced a release date for NAM 31, of March 1st, 2013. The release actually reached the LEX and STEX on March 2nd. Due to the radical changes of NAM 31, the NAM Team released a "NAM 31.1" and "NAM 31.2" to fix up the problems found in the initial release.

For the NAM 32 cycle, the team decided to implement a public pre-release system, in order to subject the mod to a broader series of tests. The first pre-release build of NAM 32, Pre-Release 1 (PR1) was made available on December 19th, 2013. The pre-release process helped the team polish the resultant full release, which came out less than a month later, on January 14th, 2014.

A Mac-only NAM 32.1 release was issued on July 28, [2014](/2014), designed to address the crash-to-desktop issues that affected some critical NAM components on the [Aspyr](/aspyr) port of SC4, which had previously led the NAM Team to drop Mac support after NAM 29. No other features were added or updated as part of that release.

After a lengthy development cycle of more than 18 months, NAM 33 finally emerged as a pre-release build on July 29th, 2015. The official release of NAM 33 went live on November 26, 2015, in time for Thanksgiving in the United States.

In sharp contrast, and following with the team's renewed commitment to smaller, more rapidfire releases, NAM 34 was released on Christmas Day 2015.

After somewhat longer release cycles for NAM 35 (2016) and NAM 36 (2017), the development of NAM 37 was a particularly long and protracted process, which took nearly 3 years of constant development and bugfixing until its official release in July [2020](/2020). NAM 37 marks the debut of a new Java-based cross-platform installer, developed by [daeley](/people/daeley) nearly a decade prior, greatly easing the process of installing the mod on macOS and Linux.

Following the frustrations with the lengthy development cycle for NAM 37, the NAM Team decided to rededicate efforts to shorten release cycle timeframes, adopting something akin to an Agile development framework. Since this change, which marked the beginning of the NAM's "Agile Era", NAM 38 was released a mere 2 months after NAM 37, on September 3, [2020](/2020), with NAM 39 released on December 3, [2020](/2020), only another 3 months out from that. NAM 40 took a languid 4 months, releasing April 1, [2021](/2021), but was quickly superseded by NAM 41 the same month, on the 17th anniversary of the release of NAM 1. NAM 42 was released on August 24, [2021](/2021), and NAM 43 4 months later, on Christmas Eve 2021.

Going into [2022](/2022), which saw its first release of the year with NAM 44 on March 22, [2022](/2022), the NAM Team had substantially grown in size, from the mere skeleton crew from the later Monolithic Era, to a size approaching that of the late-00s.

The second release of [2023](/2023), NAM 47, saw the introduction of the Draggable Classic PedMalls, a breakthrough upgrade to the Pedestrian functionality that allows access to RCI zones, the first new RHW networks since NAM 31, with the addition of the RHW-12S at ground, L1, and L2 elevations, and the X3 ramps for the RHW, among other features.

## Technical Information

<img src="/_files/images/road.jpg" title="A avenue/road interchange, released as part of a pre-NAM transit mod." width="250" alt="A avenue/road interchange, released as part of a pre-NAM transit mod." /> <img src="/_files/images/smoncrienewcrossings.jpg" title="On-slope and double-height network puzzle pieces are some highly-anticipated components included in the past few NAM versions." width="250" alt="On-slope and double-height network puzzle pieces are some highly-anticipated components included in the past few NAM versions." />

The NAM is a [RUL](/rul)-bound Plugin, in that it contains modified versions of the game's RUL Files, which control the placement and layout of transit items in-game. These RUL modifications are what allow for the numerous puzzle pieces, draggable additions and bridges that are possible with the NAM. The NAM Team serves as a community steward for the RUL files, ensuring that all RUL-bound transit content is compatible and interoperable.

One of the other oft-mentioned features of the mod are the modifications to the Traffic Simulator. The current offering since NAM 28 is the NAM Unified Traffic Simulator, developed by [z](/people/z) over several years. This simulator plugin comes in several different capacity flavors (Classic, Low, Medium, High and Ultra) and can be further customized with the Traffic Simulator Configuration Tool. It is specifically optimized for use with several NAM periphery plugins such as RealHighways and the Network Widening Mod, which require specific settings in order to function properly.
